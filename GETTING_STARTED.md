# Getting started with Rexy 360

## 1. Install

Download `Rexy360-Setup-<version>.exe` from the **[latest release](../../releases/latest)** and run it. The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows shows **"Windows protected your PC"** — click **More info → Run anyway**. Read and accept the licence, choose a folder, and start **Rexy 360**.

On first launch Rexy 360 makes **My first project** in `Documents\Rexy Studio Projects`.

## 2. Open a 360 clip

- **Import clip…** (top bar), or drag a video onto the viewer, or add files to the **Library** (right-hand panel) and double-click one.
- **From URL…** imports from YouTube, Dropbox, Google Drive or a direct link. Tick **Only part of it** and set **From / To** to download just a section of a long video. (Only download what you have the rights to use.)

A proxy builds in the background (progress at the bottom of the viewer) so scrubbing is smooth; renders always use the original.

## 3. Bind your wheels

1. Put **Rexy Wheels** in **joystick mode** and plug them in (or connect a gamepad).
2. In **Head — Pan · Tilt · Roll**, click **Bind** on Pan, then turn the pan wheel. Do the same for Tilt and Roll — and Zoom in **Lens**.
3. Choose **Absolute** (the wheel position is the angle) or **Continuous** (the wheel drives a speed, like a fluid head) under **Wheels**.
4. Fine-tune each axis with **S** (sensitivity), **C** (curve), **D** (damping) and **F** (filter). **C** next to an input opens calibration and the Filter Lab.

## 4. Operate and record a move

- Play the clip and operate. **REC** records your move as a take (a **shot**); **PLAY** plays it back.
- **I / O** set the shot's In and Out. **◆ MARK** drops marks; **✎** (E) reshapes a track with anchors.
- **⇩ Save / ⇧ Load** keep moves as `.rxmove` files.

## 5. Time, look and music

- **Time** — speed (with ramps), shutter angle (motion blur), trails, slow-motion frames.
- **Colour** — grade the shot, or load a .cube LUT.
- **Audio** — add music; **Audio → Move…** makes a move pulse or follow it.

## 6. Render

**Export — render queue**: pick a shape, size and codec (H.264, HEVC, ProRes), set the folder, and **Render now** (or queue several). Renders come from the full-resolution original, with the shot's look and sound.

## 7. Edit shots together

Switch to **EDIT** (top left). Drag shots from the Library onto the timeline, trim, add overlay tracks with blend modes, transitions on cuts (◆), music, a grade over the whole Edit — then **Render edit**.

## Updates and help
